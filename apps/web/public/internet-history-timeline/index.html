INTERNET HISTORY

Internet history, in one timeline.

The internet did not begin with social media, and the web is not the same thing as the internet. This timeline follows the major shifts that turned experimental computer networks into the searchable, social, mobile and AI-shaped internet billions of people use today.

ARPANETTCP/IPWorld Wide WebSearchSocial mediaMobile internetAI

Internet vs. World Wide Web

The internet is the global network of networks that lets computers exchange data. The World Wide Web is one system that runs on top of that network, using URLs, HTTP and linked documents. That distinction matters: email, file transfer and networked communication existed before the web, while the web later made the internet dramatically easier for ordinary people to navigate.

1969

ARPANET sends its first host-to-host message

A UCLA computer connected to the Stanford Research Institute over ARPANET, one of the foundational experiments behind networked computing.

Source: UCLA

1983

ARPANET switches to TCP/IP

The transition to TCP/IP gave different computer networks a common way to communicate. It is one of the clearest technical milestones in the emergence of the modern internet.

Source: Internet Society

1989–1991

The World Wide Web is proposed, built and opened outward

Tim Berners-Lee proposed a hypertext system at CERN in 1989. By 1991, the web software was being made available beyond CERN, setting up the web's rapid expansion.

Source: CERN

1993

Mosaic makes graphical web browsing approachable

NCSA Mosaic helped popularize the graphical browser experience, lowering the barrier to exploring the web for people who were not network specialists.

Source: NCSA

1995–1998

The commercial and searchable web takes shape

Amazon sold its first book online in 1995. Google was incorporated in 1998 after Larry Page and Sergey Brin developed a search system that used relationships between web pages as a signal. The web was becoming both a marketplace and a navigable information space.

Sources: Amazon · Google

2004–2006

The social web becomes mainstream

Facebook launched in 2004, YouTube's first video arrived in 2005, and Twitter launched in 2006. Publishing online was shifting from maintaining websites toward feeds, profiles, video uploads and continuous public conversation.

Sources: Meta · YouTube

2007

The iPhone accelerates the mobile internet

Apple introduced the first iPhone as a phone, media player and web-capable mobile device. The smartphone era moved internet use away from the desk and toward an always-connected, app-centered model.

Source: Apple

2010s

Feeds, apps, streaming and creators reshape digital culture

Instagram, Snapchat, Discord, TikTok, streaming services and creator platforms changed how online identity, entertainment and communities worked. Internet history became increasingly inseparable from everyday culture.

2020–2022

Generative AI becomes a mass-market internet experience

OpenAI introduced GPT-3 in 2020, demonstrating broad language capabilities at scale. ChatGPT launched as a research preview in 2022 and brought conversational generative AI to a mass audience.

Sources: OpenAI — GPT-3 · OpenAI — ChatGPT

Why chronology is harder than recognition

Most people recognize Google, Facebook, the iPhone or ChatGPT. The harder question is how those moments relate to one another. Did YouTube arrive before Twitter? Was Bitcoin proposed before WhatsApp launched? Did the web go public before Mosaic? Chronology exposes the mental shortcuts we use when remembering technology.
